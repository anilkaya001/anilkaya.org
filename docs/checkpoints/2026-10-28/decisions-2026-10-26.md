# Decisions closed, 2026-10-26

The six open decisions of the math audit (`docs/math-audit.md`, PR 263) and the Premium Desk's owner list (PR 265) are decided here, each by the implementer after a consultation of Opus through the API (xhigh effort), with every answer checked against the code and, where a number could be measured, against a simulation. Nothing below waits on the owner. Each entry says what shipped, what Opus said, where this tree disagreed and why, the measurement, and the tests with their planted wrong builds. No agent called the data feed; every figure comes from fixtures, invented tickers or seeded simulations, and the one thing that only a real read can settle (the feed's sort order and filter) is answered by a check the Worker runs on its own data (M-D11).

Suites: `decisions-math` (25,705 checks), `decisions-desk` (157), the extended `flows-forward-lab` (20 declared cells, 7 more planted builds), and the edited `math-vol`, `flows-tape-stats`, `flows-sides`, `flows-oracle-core`, `flows-oracle-live`, `flows-desk-sc`.

## Math audit

### 1. The Oracle's surge bar

Shipped. The bar is `max(own, 500,000)` with `own = scale x (blk + 3.5 x sqrt(blk x w))`: `blk` is the name's typical total flagged premium in a half hour (its session total over the session's half hours, 13, or 7 on an early close), `w = median x exp(1.5 s^2)` is the second moment over the first of one window (`s` from the median and p90 the ledger already stores, clamped to 1.5), and the event scale (1.5 within 21 sessions of a report) multiplies the name's own level and never the 500,000 minimum. The detector still needs three windows in the trailing thirty minutes. The label is "Bar for this name" when the name's own level binds and "Minimum bar" when the floor does, in the facts and in the words.

The ledger `oracle-norm` is version 2: an entry is `[winN, median, p90, sizeMedian, sizeP90, day, blkK, n]`, `blkK` in thousands of dollars, `n` the sessions of history (at most 99). `blk` is a winsorised exponential average: `x = sessionTotal / B`, `x' = min(x, max(4 blk, 50,000))`, `blk += max(1/n, 2/41) (x' - blk)`. A session the name did not appear in counts as zero when the name next appears (up to 20 missed sessions folded at once). A legacy six-element entry is converted on its next fold and starts at one session. A name has no surge bar, and so no surge, until it has ten sessions.

Why: the old `typical = p90 x 30/390` took a quantile of single windows for a half-hour total, so the 500,000 floor was the bar for every name with a p90 under 2.17 million. Simulated (seeded, rolling thirty-minute evaluation, 1.5 windows a block, median 266K, p90 900K): the old rule fires in 17.9% of minutes, three times the true typical total in 4.4%, the shipped rule in 0.7%; at 5 windows a block the old rule fires in 86% of minutes, at 10 in 99.6%.

| windows per block | old rule | 3 x blk | shipped (z = 3.5) | bar | episodes a session |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 1.3% | 1.1% | 0.3% | 1.84M | 0.10 |
| 1.5 | 17.9% | 4.4% | 0.7% | 3.44M | 0.19 |
| 5 | 85.7% | 1.9% | 0.8% | 7.23M | 0.21 |
| 10 | 99.6% | 0.25% | 0.7% | 11.5M | 0.19 |

Opus: the target is right (mean per half hour including empty ones) but a plain average is not robust (one 200M day lifts a 627K level to 1.35M and silences the name for three weeks), so winsorise; a fixed multiple of the mean gives very different rates across names, so add the variance term `z sqrt(blk w)`; keep the floor, stop scaling it for earnings; the label change.

Disagreed with Opus on two points. (1) Seeding a legacy entry as `(winN / 13) x median x exp(s^2/2)`: `winN` is an exponentially weighted count over about forty sessions (`keep = old x 39/40 + n`), so windows a session are `winN / 40`, not `winN / 13`; the formula would seed three times too high. Legacy names restart at one session instead and are silent for nine more. (2) z: Opus asked for the smallest z in {2.5, 3, 3.5} with at most 0.15 episodes a session. None meets that when an episode is any firing after thirty quiet minutes (0.19 at 3.5); the rule that does hold in every cell of the 12-cell grid is a rolling firing rate of at most 2% of evaluations, which z = 3.5 meets with room (worst cell about 1%) and z = 3 nearly (1.6%). Power: four extra windows at 1.5 a block are caught in 63% of sessions at z = 3.5 (78% at 3).

Tests (`decisions-math`): the worked bar, the independent formula over 200 norms, the page copy against the reference leaf over 360 comparisons, the winsorisation bound (one 200M day raises a 627K level by 14.6%), a fivefold step reaching four times the level in 29 sessions (28 without the clip), zero and missed sessions, early closes, legacy conversion, the byte cap, and the seeded simulation with four planted wrong builds (z of two, three times the level, the old single-window slice, the spread read from the median) that each push some cell past 2%. `flows-oracle-core` kills eight more, among them a floor scaled by an event, a surge with no history and a label that never says minimum.

### 2. The time basis of the priced move

Shipped: the session clock the rest of the code uses (weekend variance zero, `sess-v0` of `shared/flows-targets.js`), anchored to the window the implied volatility was struck on. The feed's 30-day volatility is the total variance of thirty calendar days. Over a horizon of `N` sessions the move is `iv30 x sqrt((30/365) x N / S30)`, where `S30` is the number of sessions in the thirty calendar days after the card's session (20 to 23, holidays included). Over the window's own sessions it is exactly `iv30 x sqrt(30/365)` for any start date (tested over 365 dates); the old `sqrt(N/252)` is 0.7% wide on a 22-session window and 3% narrow on a 20-session one. The realized figure beside it keeps `sqrt(N/252)`, because its returns are close-to-close on 252 sessions. The board's Move column and the event move to earnings use the same function (`impliedHorizonMove`), the card's text says "scaled by the sessions of its 30-day window" and its band note shows `(30/365) x N/S30`.

Opus argued against calendar time (it is 13% narrow to 44% wide on a one-session horizon over a weekend or a holiday) and for a business clock in which every non-trading date carries a tenth of a session. Agreed on the first, disagreed on the tenth. The implied move is compared with the realized one on the same card (the volatility premium, the pin reading), and the realized one is built from returns in which the weekend sits inside Monday's return and every session counts once; an implied move with a weekend weight would compare unlike things. The tree already fixes weekend variance at zero for its typical-day scale, and a tenth is a judgement no measurement here supports. The IV-days ranking unit (change over `iv30 / sqrt(252)`) stays: it is a labelled unit, not a claim about an expected move, and the 1.4% it differs from the window clock moves no order.

Tests: the window count against an independent exchange-calendar loop for nine dates, the 365-date identity, monotone growth, the 252 fallback when no date is known, the card's figures, and four planted builds (the old year, calendar days in the horizon, a calendar-day window, 31 days).

### 3. The ask-share rule against the window invariant

Shipped: one rule, the ledger's. A window whose ask and bid sides sum past its premium, compared in whole cents parsed from the decimal digits (`shared/flows-cents.js`, the ledger's own parser, half to even, rounding counted), is invalid: it states no side, is left out of the share and out of the coverage, and is counted (`invalid` on `askShare` and `askShareRead`; the ledger already counts it with its premium). The dollar split of a page that draws ask, bid and neither (`askSplit`, server and browser) returns the whole premium as neither for such a window, so the layers of the network, the floor and the river still add to the premium. The scaling by `prem / (ask + bid)` is gone everywhere, and `askShareCompat` in `shared/flows-sides.js` is `askShareRead`, so there are no longer three implementations.

Opus: ship "invalid", not scaling; compare in integer cents parsed from the strings; keep the invalid premium in the coverage denominator so that contradictory data can never score better than missing sides; allow a one-cent tolerance and take the cent off the larger side. Disagreed on two points. The tolerance: feed premiums are sums of prints priced to the cent, the parser reads digits (not `parseFloat x 100`), rounding is counted, and a tolerance would make a second class of window with a cent shaved off it; the ledger is cent-exact and has an independent BigInt oracle, and the plan says invalid. The denominator: the plan and the shipped ledger leave an invalid window out and print it with its premium (`invalidCents`); the page's coverage describes the windows that are consistent and the count says how many are not. The monotonicity argument is sound for a single percentage with no disclosure; here the disclosure is the count.

Tests: 6,000 random windows, 15% invalid, agree between the share, the ledger (`windowEntry`) and the page on which are invalid and add to the premium either way; the fixture table (`tests/fixtures-ask-share.json`) with the three scaled cases rewritten and three added (one cent over, balanced to the cent, an invalid window pooled with a valid one); four planted builds of the server and one of the page.

### 4. A materiality floor for the event premium

Shipped for the term-structure move (`assessEvent` in `shared/flows-event-variance.js`, used by the term panel through `eventMaterial`): the pure algebra is unchanged; the move is shown only when `s1^2 - s2^2 > 4 sqrt(s1^2 d1^2 + s2^2 d2^2)` (two standard errors of the difference, `d` the half-spread of each leg's volatility quote, 0.005 when the spread is not known, never under 0.0025), the front is at least a day out, the gap between the expiries at least a week, and the move at least a quarter of one percent of the price. The time terms cancel out of the test, so only the volatilities decide. Codes: `below-resolution`, `front-too-short`, `pair-too-close`, `immaterial`, each with its reason. A hundredth of a point is below resolution; the worked 35 against 30 at 7 and 37 days is 2.77% at seven times what the quotes can resolve.

Opus's specification adopted whole, including the calendar-inverted check (already the code `calendar-arbitrage`). The engine's smile-fit event (`event.*` codes) keeps its own residual and grade gates and is not changed: there a small premium is a small jump in the law, not a figure a reader sees.

Tests: the worked case, the thresholds from both sides (0.3141 and 0.3142), the scale invariance, every code, six planted builds.

### 5. Nearly constant derived series

Shipped in the primitives. A series is degenerate when `max - min <= 1e-9 x scale`, the scale being the largest magnitude by default and a caller's own when it names one: `sampleVar`, `sampleSd`, `pearson` and `spearman` take `scale` (`scaleX`, `scaleY`). Variance of a degenerate series is exactly zero, a correlation of one is null, and every z-score already refuses a zero deviation. Spearman tests the raw values before ranking. The realized-volatility estimators, the Yang-Zhang and gap variances and the volatility of log implied volatility name scale 1 (their series are log ratios, and a few ulps around zero are rounding whatever their maximum), and `flows-variation.js` carries the same rule.

Opus: put the floor in the primitives, keep 1e-9 (about two orders above the rounding of a long sum and one under the finest real price resolution), test Spearman on raw values and correlations on the complete pairs, and let the caller override the scale because a derived series (returns, a spread, a residual) is exactly where the operand scale is not the series' own. All adopted. My own argument that returns near zero are safe because the floor is relative was wrong, as Opus said: [0, 2e-16, -2e-16] has a spread equal to its maximum.

Tests: the ulp cases at three lengths and five levels, a real spread of one part in a million and in 5e8, a price of 1e5 moving a tenth of a cent, both correlations on noise, the scale override, and `math-vol`'s planted builds (an exact-equality floor, a floor of 1e-3, a Spearman that ranks noise).

### 6. AM-settled index expiries

Shipped. `shared/flows-quant-time.js`: `settlementOf(root, day)` (SPX, NDX, RUT and DJX are AM on a standard monthly, VIX and VIXW always, every other root and a missing root PM), `expiryUtcMs`, `lastTradeMs` and `yearFraction(asOfMs, day, root)`: an AM series runs to 09:30 New York on its expiry day (13:30 UTC in daylight time, 16:30 TRT), not to 16:00. The card, the engine, the desk's premium clock, the basis fit and the ledger's delta read the root from the option symbol (`rootOfSymbol`) or the alert's ticker. `buildExpiry` returns nothing for an AM series after its last trade (the close before), so a stale premium is never priced over the 17.5 hours between. A chain that holds SPX and SPXW rows on one third Friday keeps the first style and counts the other as foreign, which is the grouping trap Opus named.

Opus: price to the open (the payoff is struck on the opening quote and the holder carries the night; the close would make T reach zero while quotes still exist), keep the monthly gate, list VIX and VIXW as always AM, hold MRUT, XEO and NQX at PM until the exchange sheets are read, exclude an AM series from ranking between its last trade and its settlement, and guard the grouping. Adopted; MRUT is removed from the AM list I had first written. Not done: a run-time warning when an AM root arrives on a day that is not a standard monthly. It is priced as PM (a longer time, so a wider and more cautious figure) and the grouping guard turns a feed that mislabelled the day into a visible duplicate. Whether the feed labels the expiry by the settlement day is the one thing only a real row can show, and no agent reads one.

Tests: the five Opus time cases to the second (17.5 and 24 hours, the minute before the open, 14:30 UTC, the 6.5-hour gap between the two series), the holiday-shifted monthlies of 2025 and 2026, VIX on a Tuesday after a holiday, the last trade, the root from the symbol, and five planted builds (everyone at the close, the previous close, a weekly index root AM, VIX only on a monthly, an hour early).

## Premium Desk

### 7. The dividend calendar in the forward

Shipped. The nightly's `vx.dividends` part (ex-dates only, the amount stays withheld because the feed states no unit) reaches the desk through the card-x row the Worker already reads for the engine block; `dividendOutlook` (`shared/flows-vendor-extras.js`) turns it, for each expiry and the New York date of the read, into `declared` (an ex-date in `(today, expiry]`, with its last day to exercise), `expected` (the schedule's next date, counted only if it is more than `10 - age` days away, because a dividend needs ten days' notice and a fresh read that does not show it is not coming), `none-known`, or `unknown`; a declared date less than 0.6 of a gap after the last is a special one. In `chooseForward`:

- a declared or expected dividend inside an expiry of one gap or less moves the carry by the expected dividend `d = q x gap/365` (0.4% of the price when no yield is known) and replaces the continuous yield, with error 25% of `d` (the whole of it when the yield is unknown, half for an expected one); beyond a gap the continuous yield stands, so the two agree where they meet; a special dividend is not shifted and carries 2% of the price as error;
- the strikes where exercising a call just before the ex-date beats holding it (the hard strike, from `D x 1.5 >= K(1 - e^(-r tau)) + P(K)` on the dividend after the date) print no delta and are named in the cut as "exercise"; the soft strike, the hard one for a price 1.645 sigma higher at the ex-date, is read from the put and left out of the parity pairs;
- the early-exercise correction stays gated by evidence and the forward is not shifted for it.

Opus's design, with its numbers, adopted whole (8.3 basis points for the one-day example, the exercise strikes 99.93 at three hours). The lab: the three-hour American cell covers the truth 0.43 without the calendar and 1.00 with the ex-date declared, and the printed bands are right on all rows (1.000 plain and plus-minus in every cell, 0.5 to 0.99 dash share by tenor because every in-the-money call is withheld); the special dividend declared but unsized (a 2% European world) is a dash on 95%+ of rows up to two days and every printed band covers. Soft strike z: tried 0, 0.5, 1.0 and 1.645; coverage in the one-to-seven-day cells is 0.37 to 0.53 at z = 0, 0.90 at 0.5, 1.00 from 1.0, and parity is unused in the declared cells above 1.0, which the carry's 3 to 4 basis points of error does not miss; 1.645 stays.

Not done, with the reason: Opus's unit self-check for the dividend amount (the median of `amount / (q S gap/365)` between 0.8 and 1.25 as proof that the amount is dollars a share) would put a feed amount into the forward, and the amount is withheld by design until the feed states its unit; a real read is the only evidence and none is available. The yield-based `d` covers regular payers and the 25% error covers their raises.

### 8. Carry-only chains under two days

Shipped as Opus's rule: a chain with fewer than three clean pairs, under two days to expiry, prints at least plus or minus 0.03 (a computed half-width under that rounds up to 0.03) and a dash above 0.10 as before, with the sentence "Few matched call-put pairs: this delta rests on spot, rates and the dividend yield only, and a dividend or borrow fee missing from our data is not reflected", unless a calendar read in the last three days shows no dividend (`none-known`), which under the ten-day notice rule rules out a dividend of any size inside the week. Chains with three pairs are unaffected. With no calendar, a stale one (older than three days) or an expired one (older than seven) the cap holds. Opus's optional widening for the chance that an unseen regular dividend falls in a 3-to-7-day window is not built: where the part exists the calendar answers it, and the part is read for every carded name each night.

The one-day borrow case Opus raised (30% a year is 8 basis points, 0.02 to 0.03 of delta) sits at the edge of the plain line only for volatilities under 30% on a carry-only chain, and the new floor of 0.03 now covers it; the lab proves no plain band is printed on a carry-only chain under two days (`capCriteria`) and a planted build without the cap fails it.

### 9. The delta floor

Decided: keep a stated floor of 0.05 on the delta in the collateral (`max(h x max(delta, 0.05), f)`), never make a call ineligible below it. At the default terms (haircut 30%, floor 10%) the stated 10% binds first up to a delta of a third and the delta floor never does; it matters only when the floor is set under 1.5%, and the Terms dialog allows that. Opus: the floor is dead code at the defaults, so either delete it or state it; ineligible rows below 0.05 would hide legitimate deep out-of-the-money calls. Kept, with tests at the four points Opus named (1,000, 1,000, 1,500 and 150 at spot 100). Not built: a collateral for a call whose forward is too uncertain to give a delta, from the upper end of the delta band; a wide band exists only where the delta is steep, near the money, where the haircut times the delta is above the floor anyway. The collateral is the site's model and the page calls it so; it is not margin.

### 10. The wording of EV

Shipped: "EV (model vol)" and "Win % (model vol)", with the sentence "Win % (implied) is read from option prices and carries a risk premium. EV uses this site's volatility model, so the two differ." in the EV tooltip, disclosure and export labels. Opus: the law's drift is the forward (checked: `driftNeutralBinned` sets its mean to the forward), so only its volatility differs from the market's, and "real-world" misdescribes it; the EV is the model's volatility against the market's. The desk's copy rows, labels and tests are renamed; the rows are held to the retired-word list (no will, should, likely, forecast, predict, expect, recommend, advice, opportunity, attractive, real-world, tape).

The strategy lab, the Neuron, the dossier and the glossary still call the same law "the real-world law" in about twenty places: each defines it where it is used (the lab's copy says it is the GARCH law simulated from the name), and a rename across them is a different change from the desk's two column headers. Left as they are on purpose.

### 11. Run-time self-checks for the feed's behaviour

Shipped (`vendorCheck` in `server/desk-sc.js`, reported in `cut.vendor` and logged once per kind per isolate). No agent can read the feed's sort order or tell whether `exclude_zero_oi_chains` is honoured, so the Worker reads them from its own rows:

- the filter: honoured (`true`) with no zero open interest in 200 rows or more; ignored (`false`) when a fully read expiry of at least ten rows with strikes on both sides of the price has no open interest anywhere; unknown (`null`) otherwise, since the parameter's text ("chains with 0 open interest") may be row-level or expiry-level and a zero inside a live expiry proves nothing; the hundred-contract open-interest gate already keeps empty rows out of the ranking, so an ignored filter costs pages and an earlier truncation, not a wrong rank;
- the order: ascending, descending, by strike, mixed or single, over the expiry runs and strikes as read; a truncated read then names what it did not see ("expiries later than X", "expiries earlier than X", "strikes above S", or "unknown"); the last expiry of a truncated read is named as partly read; a read ending on a full page at its ceiling is truncated;
- duplicate symbols (a shifted page) and failed pages are counted, and the page prints the filter and order sentences beside the existing partial-chain note.

Opus's reworked signal adopted (the row-level reading would have made `zeroOi > 0` a false alarm), including the descending case being as determinate as the ascending one.

### 12. Cost controls and the kill switch

Shipped. `FLOWS_DESK_SC_PAGES` of `0`, `off` or `false` (any case, padded) switches Short calls off: `503 sc_off` before any limiter or feed call, the other routes untouched; 1 to 24 is the page ceiling (above 24 is held at 24), anything else is 12. `SC_COLD` (3 a minute, one key for the site) is asked once per unit of up to 14 feed calls for each cold read (`ceil((pages + 2) / 14)`: one at the default, two at 24 pages) and only when the 60-second snapshot is missed, so a re-rank costs none; a refusal serves the kept copy stamped throttled or a `429 rate_limited`. That caps Short calls at 42 of the 120 tokens a minute of `UW_ONDEMAND` (35%) at the default ceiling, per location. `SC_REFRESH` (1 a minute per member) is asked only for a refresh of Short calls; a refused refresh is answered from the snapshot or the stored copy with `X-Chain-Refresh: limited`. Both bindings are in `wrangler.toml` and `wrangler.staging.toml` under namespaces of their own, held by `contracts` and `staging-contract`.

Opus: cap lower than 47% because the limiter is per location and eventually consistent (two active locations double it), size the units by the page ceiling, refresh once a minute so one member cannot take every slot, an off word that survives a typo, and an error that is not a 404. All adopted. Not built: a refresh on a snapshot younger than 20 seconds answered "fresh" (the route already has a 15-second floor for a name, which does the same).
